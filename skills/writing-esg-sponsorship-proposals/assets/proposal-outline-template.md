# ESG/CSR Sponsorship Proposal — Outline Template

**Sections:** [1. Opening framing](#1-opening-framing-mission--positioning) · [2. Methodology](#2-methodology) · [3. Case-study proof point](#3-case-study-proof-point) · [4. Proposed program](#4-proposed-program) · [5. Ask / call to action](#5-ask--call-to-action) · [6. Closing](#6-closing) · [Voice check before sending](#voice-check-before-sending)

A ~70%-drafted skeleton for a corporate ESG/CSR sponsorship proposal. Fill
every bracketed placeholder from your own organization's fact sheet and the
target company's stated ESG/SDG pillars (see `writing-esg-sponsorship-proposals`
Steps 1–5) before sending. Do not leave a placeholder unfilled or invent
content to fill it — if a section has no strong real content, say so
explicitly rather than fabricating.

---

## 1. Opening framing (Mission / positioning)

> [Your organization's positioning line — the one-sentence claim that states
> what makes your organization distinctive, pulled verbatim from your fact
> sheet.]

[Headline stat block — 2-4 real, verifiable numbers from your fact sheet that
establish scale/credibility: years operating, people/sessions served,
locations, etc. Lead with credibility, not emotion.]

**Buyer role this section speaks to:** [Economic buyer / Technical buyer —
this section exists to establish credibility before the ask.]

---

## 2. Methodology

[Describe, in your organization's own terms, how your program/offering is
actually delivered: who is involved (e.g. facilitator + partner +
participant), what a typical session or engagement looks like, and what makes
your approach distinct from a generic version of the same service.]

**Buyer role this section speaks to:** [Technical buyer — this is where
measurability and rigor get established.]

---

## 3. Case-study proof point

> [Case study matched to the target's stated pillar — pull this from your
> fact sheet's case-studies section (see `building-brand-kits`). Use the real
> company/industry type, the real numbers, and the real outcome. Do not
> paraphrase the numbers into vaguer claims.]
>
> Example (illustrative, not real): "Since 20XX, [Illustrative Co.], a
> [industry] company, has partnered with us on [N] sessions reaching [N]
> participants, co-developing [specific deliverable]." — replace entirely
> with your own real case study; never present a fictional example like this
> one as if it were real.

**If no strong-fit case study exists in your fact sheet:** say so explicitly
here — e.g. "No directly comparable case study; leading with the
methodology and stat block above instead." Do not invent one to fill this
section.

**Buyer role this section speaks to:** [Economic buyer / Champion — proof
this has worked before, and ammunition for the champion to use internally.]

---

## 4. Proposed program

[Tie this directly to the SDG/ESG pillar the target company itself stated —
see the SDG/ESG pillar mapping worksheet in `writing-esg-sponsorship-proposals`.
Name the specific offering from your fact sheet that matches their pillar,
and describe the proposed scope: format, cadence, duration, audience size.]

- Target's stated pillar: `[e.g. SDG4 — quality education]`
- Your matching offering: `[the specific program/service from your fact sheet]`
- Proposed scope: `[format / cadence / duration / audience size]`

**Buyer role this section speaks to:** [User buyer — this is the section they
scrutinize for logistics and engagement fit.]

---

## 5. Ask / call to action

[State the specific next step you want the reader to take — a meeting, a
pilot session, a signed multi-year agreement — in one clear sentence. Avoid
vague asks like "let's explore possibilities."]

> [Call to action — e.g. "We'd like to schedule a 30-minute discussion to
> scope a pilot engagement for Q_ 20__."]

---

## 6. Closing

[End on the emotional payoff line — the human outcome this partnership
creates — but only here, after credibility has already been established
above. Never open the proposal with this line.]

> [Closing emotional payoff line — one sentence, specific to the outcome your
> organization actually delivers, not generic sentiment.]

**Buyer role this section speaks to:** [Champion — this is the line they'll
often quote when advocating internally.]

---

## Voice check before sending

Run the completed draft through `auditing-brand-voice` (or your own
brand-voice review process) before sending: confirm the register is
formal/credibility-led throughout, that every stat and case study traces back
to your fact sheet, and that no placeholder above was left unfilled or
fabricated.
