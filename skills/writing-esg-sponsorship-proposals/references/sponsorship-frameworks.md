# ESG & sponsorship frameworks

Reference material for grounding a sponsorship proposal in recognized ESG
reporting standards and a professional sponsorship-package structure. Use it to
mirror a target's reporting frame and to build tiered packages that sell
engagement, not just logo placement.

**Sections:** [ESG reporting frames](#esg-reporting-frames-what-the-target-reports-against) · [Finding the target's stated pillars](#finding-the-targets-stated-pillars) · [Tiered sponsorship packages](#tiered-sponsorship-packages) · [Measurement & activation](#measurement--activation) · [How this feeds the skill](#how-this-feeds-the-skill)

## ESG reporting frames (what the target reports against)

The SDG worksheet in the skill assumes the target frames its commitments as UN
Sustainable Development Goals. Many companies don't — they report against a
formal standard or their own pillar taxonomy. Mirror whichever the target
actually uses:

- **GRI Standards** (Global Reporting Initiative) — the most widely used
  sustainability-reporting system. Structured as: **Universal** standards
  (GRI 1 Foundation, GRI 2 General Disclosures, GRI 3 Material Topics),
  **Sector** standards, and **Topic** standards grouped Economic (200-series),
  Environmental (300), Social (400). A GRI reporter talks in "material topics"
  and disclosure numbers, and often maps them to SDGs.
  (https://www.globalreporting.org/standards/)
- **SASB / ISSB (IFRS S1/S2)** — investor-focused, industry-specific
  sustainability disclosure; common at large listed companies.
- **UN SDGs** — the 17 goals; the default the skill's worksheet models.
- **Company's own ESG pillars** — many firms publish 3–5 named pillars (e.g.
  "Planet / People / Governance") that don't map 1:1 to any standard. If the
  target uses these, mirror *their* pillar names verbatim.

**Rule:** lead with the target's own frame and vocabulary. If they report in
GRI material topics, echo those; if they use named pillars, use those exact
names — don't translate their language into SDGs (or vice versa) just because
your worksheet is SDG-shaped.

## Finding the target's stated pillars

If the user can't paste the ESG report excerpt, find it before drafting the
angle — don't default to a generic pitch:

1. Web-search `"[company] ESG report"`, `"[company] sustainability report PDF"`,
   or `"[company] corporate responsibility"`; fetch the latest report or the
   CSR/sustainability page.
2. Extract the named pillars / material topics / SDGs the company itself lists,
   and quote them.
3. If nothing is publicly findable, say so and ask the user — never invent a
   pillar and tailor to a guess.

## Tiered sponsorship packages

Sell tiers, not a single ask. Each tier bundles three value types plus one
exclusive benefit, so a sponsor can self-select and higher tiers feel scarce:

| | Tier 1 (entry) | Tier 2 (mid) | Tier 3 (lead/title) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visibility** (logo, mentions, reach) | Logo on shared assets | + named in press / on-site | + title billing, top placement |
| **Engagement / activation** (how they participate) | — | Employee volunteering slot | + co-created session, speaking role |
| **Measurable value** (what they can report) | Reach numbers | + participation counts | + outcome metrics for their ESG report |
| **Exclusive benefit** (one per tier, category-limited) | — | Category exclusivity | Sole title sponsor |
| **Investment** | `[price]` | `[price]` | `[price]` |

- Give each tier **one** exclusive/limited benefit — scarcity is what moves a
  sponsor up a tier.
- **Value-in-kind:** let sponsors contribute goods/services/space, not only
  cash, and price it into the tier.

## Measurement & activation

- **Activation beats placement.** A logo is worth far less than a way for the
  sponsor to *engage* the audience (an employee volunteering day, a co-branded
  session, a speaking slot). Every tier above entry should include an
  activation, not just more visibility.
- **Pre-agree the measurement.** State what you'll measure, how, and when you'll
  report it back — reach, participation counts, and (for the technical buyer)
  outcome metrics they can cite in their own ESG/GRI report. Agreeing this
  up front is what drives renewals.

- **Step 2 (angle):** pick the target's actual frame from "ESG reporting
