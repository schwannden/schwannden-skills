# Writing ESG/CSR Sponsorship Proposals

## Overview

**Core principle:** the differentiator between an accepted and a rejected
corporate CSR sponsorship proposal is *per-account tailoring* — mirroring the
**target** company's own stated ESG/SDG pillars back to them — not a polished
generic deck. This skill turns a from-scratch, multi-day proposal derivation
into a same-day tailored draft, by always pulling real case studies and stats
from your organization's own fact sheet and never inventing them.

**Recommended background:** before drafting anything, have your organization's
fact sheet on hand — a document listing your real named case studies, impact
stats, and service/program lines (the `building-brand-kits` skill can help you
build one if you don't have it). Use it as the *only* source for case studies
and numbers in this skill; never draft a case study or stat from memory or
imagination.

## When to Use

- A target company (name + industry, optionally with a public ESG/CSR-report
  excerpt or the internal contact department — HR vs. Foundation/CSR office)
  needs a tailored sponsorship proposal brief for your organization's
  ESG/CSR-eligible offering.
- You need buyer research, pitch angle, case-study selection, and objection
  prep assembled together for a new corporate lead, in one pass.
- **Not for:** consumer/beneficiary-facing content (that's a different
  register — see your own voice guidelines), a fully polished final proposal
  document (this produces a ~70% draft a human edits, not a finished
  deliverable), or post-sale account management/renewal content.

## Quick Reference — the 5 output sections

| # | Output | Source | Key constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer-role map | Inferred from company size/industry + contact dept | Map economic / user / technical buyer + champion; don't skip any role |
| 2 | Persona-tailored ESG/SDG angle | Target's own stated CSR/ESG pillars (from excerpt or public report) | Mirror *their* pillar language — never lead with your own generic pitch |
| 3 | Best-fit case study | Your organization's own fact sheet | Must be a real case study from your fact sheet, or explicitly "no strong fit" — never fabricated |
| 4 | ~70%-drafted proposal outline | Your organization's standard proposal structure | Drafted enough to edit, not to author from scratch |
| 5 | Objection responses | Tagged by buyer role | Not optional — slow/generic responses lose deal momentum |

## Step 1 — Buyer-role map

For most corporate CSR/ESG sponsorship decisions, map all four roles; don't
collapse them:

| Role | Typical holder | What they need to hear |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic buyer** (controls budget) | Foundation/CSR office director, or CFO for large multi-year sponsorships | Cost-per-impact, multi-year partnership framing, credibility (named case studies + stat block) |
| **User buyer** (their team runs/attends the program) | HR/People team (employee-facing programs) or the business unit hosting an on-site session | Ease of scheduling, employee/audience engagement fit, low logistics burden |
| **Technical buyer** (evaluates rigor/measurability) | ESG/sustainability reporting team, or a CSR-office analyst who has to justify the line item internally | Measurable outcomes, methodology credibility, reporting deliverables they can cite in their own ESG report |
| **Champion** (internally advocates) | Whoever initiated contact — often HR or a CSR-office staffer with a personal stake | Needs ammunition to sell it upward — give them the stat block + case study as a leave-behind |

If the contact department is known (HR vs. Foundation/CSR office), start
there — HR contacts usually double as user buyer/champion; Foundation/CSR
office contacts usually double as economic buyer.

## Step 2 — Persona-tailored SDG/ESG angle

**Discipline: mirror the target's own stated pillars — never project your own
agenda onto them.** Read the target's stated CSR/ESG pillars from the
provided excerpt or their public ESG report/CSR page first, then pick the
matching angle. Mirror whichever *frame* the target actually reports in — UN
SDGs, **GRI material topics**, SASB/ISSB, or the company's own named pillars —
in *their* vocabulary, not translated into SDGs just because the worksheet
below is SDG-shaped. See `references/sponsorship-frameworks.md` for the frames
and how to distinguish them.

If no excerpt is provided and the pillars are unclear, don't guess and default
to a generic pitch — find them: web-search `"[company] ESG report"` /
`"[company] sustainability report"` / `"[company] corporate responsibility"`,
fetch the latest report or CSR page, and quote the pillars/material topics the
company itself lists. Only if nothing is publicly findable, ask the user (see
the reference's "Finding the target's stated pillars").

Use the worksheet below to map *your own* offerings against common SDG/ESG
pillars — fill this in once from your fact sheet, then reuse it per account:

**SDG/ESG pillar mapping worksheet (fill in from your own fact sheet):**

| Common pillar the target might state | Which of your offerings/programs to lead with | Why it fits (your own stat/proof point) |
|---|---|---|
| SDG3 — Good health & well-being | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| SDG4 — Quality education | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| SDG8 — Decent work & economic growth | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| SDG10 — Reduced inequalities | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| SDG17 — Partnerships for the goals | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| GRI material topic (e.g. GRI 403 — Occupational health & safety) | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| Company's own named pillar (mirror their exact wording, e.g. "People & Wellbeing") | `[your offering]` | `[your stat]` |
| (add rows for any other pillar or frame your organization's fact sheet supports) | | |

**Example (illustrative, not real):** if a target's ESG report emphasizes
SDG4 (quality education), and your fact sheet lists a training/certification
program with measurable completion or skill-transfer numbers, lead with that
program and cite the completion stat up front — not a generic "we support
education" line.

## Step 3 — Case study match

Match by industry/size/similarity to the target, pulled **only** from your
own fact sheet's case-studies section:

- **Large enterprise, long-horizon partnership interest** → your fact sheet's
  longest-running or largest-scale case study, with real session/participant
  counts and the year the partnership began.
- **Financial institution or regulated industry, inclusion/well-being angle**
  → a case study that pairs your program with the target's own
  volunteer/staff resources, if you have one.
- **Foundation-style or insurance-style long-term funder** → a case study that
  demonstrates how a long-term partnership evolved over multiple years.
- **No good industry/size fit** → say so explicitly in the brief ("no strong
  case-study match; recommend leading with the stat block and methodology
  instead"). **Never fabricate a case study — if there's no strong fit among
  your organization's real case studies, say so explicitly rather than
  inventing one.**

If you don't yet have a fact sheet with a case-studies section, build one
first (see `building-brand-kits`) rather than improvising case studies here.

## Step 4 — ~70%-drafted proposal outline

Use your organization's standard proposal structure; draft each section
enough that a human edits rather than authors from scratch. See
`assets/proposal-outline-template.md` for the fillable skeleton. Section
order:

1. **Mission/positioning** — open with your organization's real positioning
   line and headline stat block (pulled verbatim from your fact sheet, not
   paraphrased).
2. **Methodology** — describe how your program is actually delivered (who's
   involved, what happens in a session), in your organization's own terms.
3. **Case study proof** — the Step 3 match, with real numbers, not
   paraphrased into vaguer claims.
4. **Proposed program** — tie directly to the Step 2 angle (whichever pillar
   the target stated, propose the offering you mapped to it).
5. **Ask** — close on a clear, specific call to action; end on the emotional
   payoff line last — credibility first, emotion last, never the reverse.

Structure the proposed program and ask as **tiered sponsorship packages**, not
a single flat amount — each tier bundling Visibility + Engagement/activation +
Measurable value, with one exclusive/category-limited benefit per tier and
pre-agreed measurement the technical buyer can cite in their own ESG report.
Sell *activation* (a way for the sponsor to engage the audience), not just logo
placement. See the tier template in `references/sponsorship-frameworks.md`.

## Step 5 — Objection responses (not optional)

Tag each by the buyer role it defuses. Fill in your own anchors from your
fact sheet:

| Objection | Buyer role | Response anchor |
|---|---|---|
| "How do we justify the cost?" | Economic buyer | Multi-year partnership value (your longest-running case study), cost-per-impact against your headline reach stat |
| "How do we measure impact for our ESG report?" | Technical buyer | Your methodology + session/participant counts as reportable metrics; offer a post-program stat summary as a deliverable |
| "Will our employees/audience actually engage with this?" | User buyer / champion | Point to a case study with a working precedent (e.g. a volunteer or staff co-delivery model); note any low-barrier-to-entry framing that applies |

## Voice check before finalizing

Draft in a formal, credibility-led corporate/ESG-B2B register: SDG/ESG
vocabulary, named case study + stat block up front, closes on emotional
payoff but never opens with it. Then run the full draft through
`auditing-brand-voice` — or your own brand-voice review process — before
sending.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Producing a generic, one-size-fits-all pitch deck | Re-derive the angle from the target's *own* stated ESG pillars (Step 2) — per-account tailoring is what separates accepted from rejected proposals |
| Inventing a case study or stat not in your fact sheet | Only cite real case studies from your own fact sheet; if nothing fits, state "no strong case-study match" instead of fabricating one |
| Using a warm, emotional-first consumer register for a B2B proposal | Use the corporate/ESG-B2B register — credibility-led, emotional payoff only at the close |
| Skipping or shortening the objection-response section | Always produce all buyer-role-tagged objections (Step 5) — slow/generic responses lose deal momentum; this is a spec requirement, not optional polish |
| Drafting without the excerpt/CSR pillar info | Ask for the target's public ESG report or CSR page first (Step 2) rather than guessing a pillar and mistailoring the angle |
| Assuming a title (e.g. "COO") maps to a specific buyer role by default | Confirm who actually holds budget/usage/technical/champion responsibility for this account rather than inferring purely from title |
